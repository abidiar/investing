# External verification audit — broad “quality earnings selloff, wait ~D4/5, buy call for rebound” strategy
Date: 2026-10-11. **Verdict: constituent stock-return mechanisms supported by peer-reviewed studies, but the combined D4/5 event + ~$50 call execution strategy is NOT verified.** This is literature and feasibility review; no new backtest or scanner change.

## Exact desired conjunction
(A) Quality company screened from publications BEFORE report; (B) earnings released AFTER HOURS, close price falls >=5% in first 1–2 RTH sessions; (C) subsequent lows on trading days 1–3 after D0 and entry around D4–D5 AS-OF not hindsight trough; (D) partial +3% underlying rebound BEFORE −5% stop within 20 sessions; (E) affordable OTM call premium actually profits at sellable BID net spread/IV/theta/DTE and fees. No study found verifying all A–E. Retrospective eventual “price recovered after months” is not C–E.

## Verified published evidence — supports some mechanisms, refutes assumption of universality
- Das, Kim & Patro (2012), *On the Anomalous Stock Price Response to Management Earnings Forecasts*, J Business Finance & Accounting, https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-5957.2012.02298.x: after BAD forecast, initial reaction sometimes reversal/upward drift, after-estimated-cost modeled profitability, no exact D4 call.
- Josef Fink (2021), *A review of Post Earnings Announcement Drift*, Journal of Behavioral & Experimental Finance, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbef.2020.100446: PEAD commonly CONTINUES in direction of actual earnings surprise for weeks/months. Correct earnings SURPRISE is analyst expectation adjusted, not YOY company figures.
- Aboody, Lehavy, Trueman (2010), *Limited attention and the earnings announcement returns of past stock market winners*, Review of Accounting Studies, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11142-009-9104-9: stocks that rallied most in preceding 12 months subsequently LOST on average 1.86% market adjusted in first 5 trading days after earnings; verified outside-hours subsample -3.05%, and returns remained negative ~through 13th session. STRONG counterexample to universal D4 buy and highlights previous 12m runup important control before cherry-picking supposedly “strong companies”. This is NOT an earnings price drop-filtered quality strategy either.
- Bathke, Mason & Morton (2019), *Investor Overreaction to Earnings Surprises and Post-Earnings-Announcement Reversals*, Contemporary Accounting Research, https://doi.org/10.1111/1911-3846.12491: if quarterly seasonal earnings change persistence is genuinely low, investors may over-extrapolate and prices reverse around following QUARTER’S earnings report, often far too late for 2-week calls. “Fundamental persistence” may be useful if measured ex ante.
- Contreras (2020), *Strategic timing of corporate insiders when trading at earnings announcements*, Finance Research Letters 34, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2019.07.015: observed insider purchases and sales between earnings D0 and D5; buying following NEGATIVE initial reaction associated with HIGHER subsequent return, selling with LOWER, and profitable insider transactions studied. **Closest independent empirical lead explicitly matching our window and overshoot issue**; importantly company insiders’ transaction dates are often NOT known to public until SEC Form 4 submission. For a feasible strategy use historical original SEC accepted filing timestamp at or before the purchase decision, not trade day, and restrict to open-market code P rather than stock awards, exercises or announced prearranged automatic trades. The paper does NOT establish that a retail trader using filings known D3–D5 makes money on OTM calls.
- U.S. SEC Official Form 4 General Instructions https://www.sec.gov/about/forms/form4data.pdf: most reportable insider transactions must be filed before end of SECOND BUSINESS DAY following execution. This delayed reporting may leave very few reported buys knowable by D3 even if purchases happened D1–D3. The research could wrongly presume advance knowledge of insider trade if only event trade date used.
- Chordia et al. (2009), *Liquidity and the Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift*, Financial Analysts Journal 65, https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/liquidity-and-post-earnings-announcement-drift: 70–100% of paper profits of the classic *earnings surprise momentum LONG/SHORT stock* are consumed by stock liquidity and market impact costs, underscoring execution frictions, though this is not the exact rebound strategy.
- *Earnings announcements and option returns*, Journal of Empirical Finance (2017), https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927539816300743: post-earnings option **straddle** return behavior differs, selling straddles post event with buying pre-earnings portfolio yields significant average hedge return, consistent with volatility risk premium / overestimation of near-term IV AFTER event. It is NOT directional long calls after sharp gap downs and not directly proof of losses on calls. STRIKE/expiry/delta/IV/risk matter.
- Published 2026 Columbia Law School commentary on study of insider BUY signal, https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2026/09/22/when-are-insider-purchases-credible-signals-of-private-information/ indicates short-term positive abnormal market reaction in 5 days after Form4 filing, but distinct general insider study, not AH D4 quality rebound and still filing-date-aware.
- 2026 *Warp speed price moves: Jumps after earnings announcements*, arxiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08962 analyzes high-frequency afterhours price jumps; postevent trading strategy consistent with efficient price formation after 2016, caution extrapolating older patterns into modern liquid mega caps.

## Next meaningful research discrimination (NOT an already passed test)
Consider previously overlooked, observable insider OPEN MARKET BUYING “management puts money to work following earnings selloff” with original SEC Form4 transaction dates **and actual acceptance timestamps <= D3/D4**, plus appropriately matched no-buy peers. Study must predefine base issuer panel across report sessions and issuer independence, exclude timed-out future disclosures, check sparse coverage and ascertain eventual option affordable asks/bids. Causal caution: insider trades may be subject to insider-trading blackout policies and trading plans; no Form4 buy is NOT equivalent to no insider optimism. Before entering any D4 call, 1) past pre-announcement stock run-up matters, 2) seasonality of EPS change/persistence, 3) actual company forward outlook vs prior expectation rather than static quality, 4) reported insider buys known in time, 5) real option premiums and exit spread.
Verdict: broad premise of occasional quality-firm postearnings recovery IS observable, not unique; predictable, economically profitable day4/5 ~$50 CALL buying under our exact conditions IS NOT independently verified. Literature offers competing explanations and a particularly attractive new insider-vs-no-insider measurement with potential SEC public free archival data, not proven alpha.
