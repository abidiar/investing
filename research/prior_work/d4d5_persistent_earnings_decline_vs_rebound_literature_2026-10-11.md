# Literature review — D4–D5 earnings decline: temporary overshoot or continuing repricing?
Search date: 2026-10-11. **STATUS: PRIOR-WORK REVIEW / NO NEW STRATEGY TEST OR PREDICTED EDGE.**

## Exact decision question
Among companies whose stocks fall sharply after an AFTER-HOURS quarterly report and often print an additional low within the first 2–5 trading sessions, can AS-OF-D3/D4 information distinguish a subsequent recoverable rebound from a continued fundamental decline? This is specifically an options-relevant **+3% rebound-before-−5% stop over ~20 trading sessions** question, not a 30–120-day calendar PEAD factor strategy. No paper discovered directly proves a robust profitable long-call entry exactly D4/D5.

## High-quality academic and related studies
1. **Analyst revisions (peer-reviewed)** — *Analyst responsiveness and the post-earnings-announcement drift*, Journal of Accounting and Economics 46(1), 2008, pp. 201–215, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacceco.2008.04.004. Uses analyst revisions within two trading days to distinguish early vs late incorporation of current news; responsive revision rates vary and more stock reaction is in event window, less in subsequent drift. **Information likely available by D4: time-stamped individual FY1/Q1 revisions, if accessible.** But it does not prove any 4th-day reversal trade.
2. **Analyst revision direction vs raw reported surprise (doctoral dissertation, NOT journal proof)** — Huang (2022), *The Value of Analyst Forecast Revisions: Evidence from Earnings Announcements*, https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2r7980f3. Aggregate post-announcement forecast revisions have more predictive power for future postearnings drift than standalone historical earnings surprise measures; when revisions contradict earnings surprise the mismatch is particularly informative. **Closest qualitative candidate for 'stock down but future expectations stable or improving'**, but need actual before/after FY1 forecasts time-aligned to D3; absence of forecasts is UNKNOWN.
3. **Risk: negative earnings news often CONTINUES downward** — Fink (2021), *A review of the Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift*, Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Finance 29, 100446, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbef.2020.100446. Direction of drift usually follows standardized earnings surprise for weeks/months; not all declines mean oversold rebound.
4. **Institutional investor information (peer-reviewed, but post-D4 measurement cannot be used as D4 feature)** — O. & D. Klein (2024), *Institutional consensus after earnings announcements: Information or crowding?*, International Review of Financial Analysis 95, 103355, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.irfa.2024.103355. In the sample, strong negative institutional consensus with bad releases corresponded to 4.78pp worse event-window cumulative abnormal returns during days 0–10 and no subsequent drift in days 11–128, consistent with accurate early fundamental repricing; investor horizon moderates overreaction. **CRITICAL: the published CAR(0;10) and observed flow over that window are POST D4 and must not be leaked into D4 screening.** High-level mechanism only until genuine point-in-time contemporaneous flow snapshots are available.
5. **Order-flow/bid-ask market mechanics (peer-reviewed UK 2000–2021; NOT US D4 call proof)** — Zhang, Gregoriou & Wu (2024), *Asymmetric post earnings announcement drift and order flow imbalance: The impact on stock market returns*, International Review of Financial Analysis 94, 103316, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.irfa.2024.103316. Links PEAD asymmetry with order-flow imbalance and bid-ask effects; paper observes short-term changes in bad-news selling pressure. Generalizes cautiously across countries, and volume alone ≠ buyer-initiated order flow.
6. **Combined features/outcome validation (working paper, NOT D4 study)** — Ye & Schuller (2020), *Capturing dynamics of post-earnings-announcement drift using genetic algorithm-optimised supervised learning*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03094. 1,106 historical Russell 1000 stocks 1997–2018 with fundamentals, standardized surprises, moving-average/momentum metrics and short-interest data; predicts 30-day cumulative abnormal returns using XGBoost; out-of-sample ranking showed relative differentiation by issuer sector and quarter, but authors flag real execution slippage. This is a more relevant design direction (multi-factor risk segmentation) than stacking ad-hoc bullish candles, but 30-day relative returns and stock portfolios ≠ 3%-before-5% stops or calls.
7. **Macro and industry context (peer-reviewed)** — Pan, Sul & Wang (2026), *Firm-specific information processing and the delayed discovery of macroeconomic news: evidence from earnings announcement returns*, Review of Accounting Studies, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11142-026-09959-y. For earnings reporters 1998–2022, announcement-day market return predicts subsequent 3-trading-day abnormal returns, strongest for extreme surprises, effect dissipates by ~day7. Distinguish broad market move from issuer-specific repricing, but do not assume effect extends profitably through D4–D20.
8. **Limits of tradeability (peer-reviewed)** — Chordia et al. (2009), *Liquidity and the Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift*, Financial Analysts Journal 65, https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/liquidity-and-post-earnings-announcement-drift. Return patterns concentrated in illiquid names; paper estimates 70–100% of paper profits from PEAD long-short strategy consumed by transaction costs. At the user's ~$50/deep-OTM call budget bid/ask and IV/expiry may dominate predicted tiny underlying edges.
9. **Contrary/current-model caution (2026 working paper, NOT settled peer-reviewed)** — Hosseinkhani (2026), *Earnings Surprise Returns or Expected Compensation? Decomposing Announcement Reactions and Post-Earnings Drift*, https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6440878. Event-level adjustment across 238,529 sample events 2000–2024 attributes 60-day drift to risk expected returns, leaving little abnormal residual; emphasizes apparent drift may not be free alpha.
10. **Different horizon reversal evidence (not a D4 prediction)** — Bathke, Mason & Morton (2019), *Investor Overreaction to Earnings Surprises and Post-Earnings-Announcement Reversals*, Contemporary Accounting Research, https://doi.org/10.1111/1911-3846.12491. Documents reversals around NEXT quarterly release among stocks with uncorrelated seasonal earnings changes, NOT guaranteed recovery days 4–5. Do not misinterpret.

**Research verdict:** Existing empirical work establishes plausible *mechanisms and candidate predictive features* but NOT an as-of-D4,D5 winning call strategy. Most promising unused measurable feature family = actual dated forward analyst revisions (with a substantial data access bottleneck).
