# Prior-work review: after-hours earnings selloff, multi-day secondary decline, then first stabilization (2026-10-11)

## The question (before method freeze)
Among companies reporting earnings AFTER the close that subsequently suffer a >=5% observed regular-session selloff, can an **early** post-event failure to keep making lower lows, followed by buyers raising the daily close, yield a better *trade entry* than simply buying a fixed later session? Distinguish late partial recovery (e.g., >=5% from selloff closing price) from an executable long-stock or affordable-call trade.

## Source classes and directly applicable findings
1. **Published academic review** — Fink (2021), *A review of the Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift* (Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Finance), DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbef.2020.100446. Reviews continuation following earnings surprises; negative news can produce continued negative drift. Thus don't assume that all gaps are 'overreactions' or that a higher-low event reverses information-based repricing.
2. **Published academic counterevidence** — Bathke, Mason & Morton (2019), *Investor Overreaction to Earnings Surprises and Post-Earnings-Announcement Reversals*, https://doi.org/10.1111/1911-3846.12491. Reports reversals under certain seasonal-earnings structures, showing continuation is not universal. Mechanism depends on whether news encodes persistent future earnings.
3. **Published transaction-friction evidence** — Chordia et al. (2009), *Liquidity and the Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift*, https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/liquidity-and-post-earnings-announcement-drift. Drift returns are concentrated in illiquid shares with heavy trading costs; reported 70–100% of paper long-short gains can be absorbed by transaction costs. Cannot equate a raw return difference with an executable option edge.
4. **Published forecasts mechanism** — *Analyst Responsiveness and the Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift* (Journal of Accounting and Economics, 2008), https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/analyst-responsiveness-and-post-earnings-announcement-drift. Faster analyst earnings forecast revisions move more information into the event window, reducing subsequent drift. Supports considering point-in-time guidance/forward-estimate change as contextual explanation, not using unknown analyst updates at entry.
5. **Recent journal work** — Lan et al. (2024), *Post earnings announcement drift: A simple earnings surprise measure, the medium effect of investor attention and investing strategy*, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521924003922. Asymmetry between positive and negative surprises and attention; one generic symmetric earnings-return rule is ill-advised.
6. **Reproducible open source example** — https://github.com/aladinbouddat/PEAD-Strategy . Event-driven SUE surprise factor and CRSP/IBES data (not openly included), cost-model long-short design spanning 1994–2023. It is **not** an AH delayed lower-low + stabilization + next-open options study, and proprietary source dependencies prevent a direct free rerun. Another open example https://github.com/PurujitAggarwal/PEAD_strategy tests after-hours announcement momentum, discloses missing raw bars and ignored frictions. Neither establishes the user's specific long CALL timing rule.
7. **Existing Investing OS evidence (strongly relevant internal test data)** — `research/earnings_recovery/after_hours_delayed_dip_recovery_diagnostic_2026-10-11.md`. In 91 selected AH earnings selloffs, 62 made a lower low within five subsequent sessions, 42 subsequently touched +5% above the initial selloff close in D6–D60. Another low is common but is NOT itself positive predictive evidence. Crucially, existing late multi-rising-low and SMA5-reclaim entries did NOT stably improve matched timing across two 92/72 historical groups; 2026 same-day 5m buyer tests were a different question and should not be recycled as refutation of multi-day recovery.

## What is unresolved and worth testing once
Do we get better risk-adjusted FOLLOW-ON stock moves if we act **at the first ONE-day seller failure plus daily buyer response** in D2–D10 following a known further low, rather than buying all new lows blindly or waiting for three higher lows? Is the signal early enough that the next opening price remains close to a known trough, and does it add value on the same earnings event compared to fixed day-six entry? This exact fast one-day morphology + delayed AH cohort has not been evaluated in the archived Investing OS record.

## Important limitations and non-goals
- Current 91 AH events came from quality-qualified earnings selloffs over 2023–2026 selected from today's stock list; quality-at-event, issuer survivorship, earnings release time accuracy, price adjustment, and same-company event clustering matter.
- The previously saved bar arrays contain OHLC but NOT signed order flow or volume. One-day higher low plus green/higher close is a price proxy for prospective buyer initiative, **not** direct volume or aggressor-order evidence.
- Post-event **negative earnings drift** can continue after a cosmetic one-day bounce. Enter at next-session OPEN, not at hindsight daily low/trigger close.
- A stock +5%/-5% target test is not equivalent to an OTM call ROI: the user needs obtainable <$50-ish premiums, as-of IV, spread, listed expiry, delta, and realistic fills; don't retrofit call expiry to historical stock path.
- Do NOT reoptimize SMA5, three-rising-lows, same-day multi-green patterns, day-3 stop widths or the 164 existing outcomes to manufacture promotion.
- Exact external reproduction of AH multi-day reversal using nonlicensed tick option quotes not found; no evidence that an automatic ~80% options win rate exists.

## Implication for next protocol
Freeze one *single-day* observable stabilization trigger, eligible only after an AH reaction day and objectively observed secondary low, test against fixed-day entries across ALL initially qualified AH cases (no triggers counted as zero), and split original vs company-disjoint cohorts. Label its 2023–2026 evaluation **retrospective/exploratory** regardless of result because earlier outcomes were already inspected. Only a truly unseen time window can support promotion. Keep option price testing a separate stage after stock baseline clears.
